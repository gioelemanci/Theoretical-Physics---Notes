\section{Basic Model for Effective Attraction}

consider a cubic lattice of positively charged ions, with a lattice spacing $a$, and a gas of electrons moving in the lattice. The electrons interact with the ions through the Coulomb interaction, and they also interact with each other through the Coulomb interaction. Electrons move in a periodic potential created by the ions, and the minima are near the ions, where the electrons are more likely to be found.

The electrons can also interact with the lattice vibrations i.e. phonons (collective vibrations, many modes), which can lead to an effective attraction between electrons. Phonons are not localized, they are collective excitations of the lattice, and they can mediate an effective attraction between electrons. The effective attraction is due to the fact that when an electron moves through the lattice, it distorts the lattice and creates a region of positive charge that can attract another electron. This is known as the electron-phonon interaction.

If we consider that the Debye frequency $\omega_D$ is around 100K and we are working at temperatures around 1K, then the phonons are frozen out and we can consider only the electrons, or we can consider the phonons as a perturbation to the electron system.

Furthermore we do not consider external magnetic or electric fields.


Electrons feel the Coulomb repulsion since \(V_{e-e} \sim \frac{e^2}{r}\), but they also feel the attraction of the positively charged background, which is the lattice of ions. This activates screening, which is a many body effect that reduces the effective Coulomb repulsion between electrons. The effective interaction between electrons can be written as \(\sim e^{-\frac{r}{\xi}}\), where \(\xi\) is the screening length, which usually is of the order of the lattice spacing \(a\) (a couple). But even if it decays exponentially, it is still repulsive, and we need to find a way to have an effective attraction between electrons. This is where the electron-phonon interaction comes into play.
